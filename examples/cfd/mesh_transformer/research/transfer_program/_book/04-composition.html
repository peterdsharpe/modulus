4  Local-response composition

Composing exact local port responses into new assemblies is exact, but errors in the smooth part of a local response are amplified 4–30x by the interface solve and grow superlinearly with assembly size on grids, while errors in the rough part are damped 20–100x; port reduction that transfers to new shapes compresses nothing at the 1% level. The audit’s third direction proposed learning local Dirichlet-to-Neumann responses in compatible port coordinates and enforcing global compatibility numerically, so that a new arrangement of familiar parts is not a new shape. The nonlearned gate asked how interface-solve error propagates when local responses are inexact, before any predictor is trained. The answer rejects the direction as proposed and points at a narrower hybrid.

4.1 The gate

Linear finite elements on unit-square components with identical straight ports (64 perimeter nodes, 15 interior nodes per shared edge), a library of five shapes (plain, coefficient contrast, hole, wide and narrow internal channels) and three held-out shapes (offset hole, diagonal barrier, an eight-cell checkerboard); assemblies in chains and grids of 2, 4, 8 and 16 components, five random draws each, against a monolithic finite-element reference; a screened variant as control. The exact machinery passes every check: response symmetry, positive semidefiniteness, the constant null vector, the Schur-complement composition law against a merged-mesh Schur complement (2 × 10⁻¹⁶), and an exact interface solve (5 × 10⁻¹⁵ energy error). Every component response is then perturbed by a relative Frobenius error ε (random, or concentrated on smooth or on rough port modes), and reduced responses are built by transfer-optimal port modes and by equal-rank proper orthogonal decomposition. Bars, written before the run (studies/composition/PREREG.md): worth a learner if the global energy error stays below 5% at ε = 10⁻² from 2 to 8 components with no faster than square-root growth, and if transfer-optimal reduction reaches 1% at a quarter of the port dimension on new assemblies of new shapes; not worth it if local errors amplify superlinearly or the interface solve costs more than the monolithic one.

4.2 What the gate found

At ε = 10⁻² the 5% bar is missed for chains of 8 or more and for every grid; from 4 to 16 components the grid error grows 6.2x, faster than linear. The mechanism is in the last two columns: errors on rough port modes are attenuated 20–100x by the interface solve, errors on smooth modes are amplified 4–30x and set the global error, because the smooth, small-eigenvalue part of a response is the load-bearing part (truncating the response itself to fewer than 63 of 64 modes gives errors of 10² to 10⁷). Port reduction confirms it from the other side: transfer-optimal modes built from a plain reference pair do transfer to new shapes, but need 13–15 of 15 modes for 1%, while proper-orthogonal modes need all 15 on new shapes (they are shape-specific); at rank 4 both leave 16–27% error. The interface solve is cheaper than the monolithic one only up to four components, and the partition-of-unity coarse space does not reduce the interface iteration count. The narrow-channel family did not exercise a near-port singularity (the barrier sits inside the component), which is a limitation of this instrument, not a finding.

4.3 What this changes

As proposed, a learned local response would need a relative error near 10⁻³ on smooth port modes to reach 2% on a 4 × 4 assembly, at full port rank, with reciprocity, positive semidefiniteness and the null vector preserved exactly; that is a stricter target than any learned response in the literature reaches, and the direction stops in that form. What survives is a hybrid: keep the smooth low-mode response exact (a coarse local solve or an analytic expansion) and learn only the rough remainder, whose errors the interface solve damps. That is a solver with a learned component, not a field regressor, and it is the same shape as the audit’s exterior variant (analytic multipole coupling, learned near-field maps) and as the wall-law and constraint-solve hypotheses of Chapter 6. Its next nonlearned test is a split response (exact low modes plus a perturbed remainder) and a barrier-adjacent-to-port family for the near-field claim; neither is launched here. The instrument is 2D scalar diffusion with shared straight ports; convection removes the energy-norm certificate, elasticity adds rigid modes per component, and exterior aerodynamics has no shared ports at all.
