6  What classical solvers do that surrogates do not

Classical PDE and CFD solvers generalize to new geometries and conditions because almost nothing in them is fitted to a geometry: the physics enters as a local operator, the geometry enters as a mesh and a set of boundary conditions, and the global coupling is produced at run time by iteration and by exact algebra. A learned surrogate inverts every one of those choices at once: it fits the global map on a family, encodes geometry implicitly, and produces the answer in one pass. The audit’s four directions are each an attempt to hand one solver ingredient back to mathematics. This chapter lists the ingredients, states what each buys and what it costs, and derives from each a hypothesis that an experiment in this program can reject. Papers cited in the audit’s two literature dossiers are referenced by name; the mechanism claims here are classical numerical analysis, not new results.

6.1 The inventory

Ingredient of a classical solver Why it generalizes What it costs What the surrogate does instead
Local discrete operator (finite volume / element stencils) The same few-point relation holds at every cell of every mesh; geometry enters only through cell metrics A sparse system whose solve is the whole cost Fits a global input→output map on one family; the operator is implicit and family-specific
Iterative solve with a residual Convergence is monitored against the equation, so a new geometry is solved to the same tolerance as a familiar one Tens to thousands of operator applications One pass; no residual, no stopping criterion, error unknown until compared to a label
Multigrid / coarse spaces Smooth error is corrected on a coarse representation that exists for any geometry; the slow space is generic smoothness Hierarchy construction; a few percent of a solve Fixed latent size chosen by validation error; the interior spectrum is whatever training produced (Chapter 5 shows source-fitted spaces lose to a free coarse grid)
Domain decomposition and static condensation Local solves plus a small exact interface system; a component’s response is reusable in any assembly with compatible interfaces Interface solve; coarse space for scalability Whole-shape regression; a new arrangement of familiar parts is a new shape
Boundary integral methods The free-space kernel is universal; geometry enters through a dense but well-understood boundary operator Dense or fast-multipole-accelerated boundary solve; only for linear homogeneous operators Learns kernel and inverse together, on a family; the repository’s exact-kernel branch transferred across boundary frequencies where a learned decoder did not
Fast multipole and translation operators Far-field interactions between separated bodies are analytic expansions; only near-field is geometry-specific Expansion order; near-field corrections for close bodies Attention over all tokens learns near and far interactions alike from data
Pressure–velocity coupling (projection, SIMPLE) The pressure solve enforces a global constraint (mass conservation) exactly at every step A Poisson solve per step Pressure is predicted pointwise; conservation is not enforced (the interior chapter of the program book reports pressure as a gauge quantity with no constraint)
Wall models and near-wall resolution The near-wall region is modelled by a local law (log law, wall function) that is the same on every wall Mesh grading or a modelled first cell A uniform token budget; the audit’s harmonic-disk spectrum control shows wall flux needs modes the interior does not
Closure models (RANS, hybrid RANS–LES) Turbulent stresses are local algebraic or transport relations, calibrated once, applied everywhere Model error; closure-dependent labels The surrogate must learn the closure’s imprint from labels; the eddy-viscosity gap (1.26x, program book) may be exactly this
Newton / Jacobian-based nonlinear solves Local linearization gives quadratic convergence near a solution on any geometry Jacobian assembly or its action; globalization No state to linearize around; branch and regime are not represented (regime extrapolation fails ~10x for every architecture tested)
Continuation in a parameter A solution at one condition initializes the next; branches are tracked, not extrapolated One solve per step Interpolation in condition space from labels; stall boundaries are not tracked
Mesh adaptation driven by error indicators Resolution follows the solution’s own difficulty Indicator evaluation and remeshing Sampling density fixed by the dataset; the program’s density-robustness tests show large sensitivity in some configurations
Nondimensionalization and scaling Inputs enter at unit scale by construction; conditioning is controlled None Left to the recipe; a dimensional freestream input produced a 2.8x artifact in this program (UDRV)
Conservation and compatibility enforced by construction Fluxes balance across faces; Neumann data are compatible Discretization design Enforced only if the architecture is built for it; ISLA enforces symmetry contracts, not conservation

Two patterns run through the table. First, classical solvers separate what is local and universal (the operator, the closure, the wall law) from what is global and case-specific (the solution), and produce the second from the first by iteration or exact algebra at run time. Surrogates learn the global object directly, so they must see the family they are asked about. Second, solvers carry an error estimate in the residual, so they can spend more work where a case is harder; surrogates spend a fixed amount everywhere and report no uncertainty. The audit’s mechanisms chapter adds the caution that a small residual is not a small error without a stability bound, so the residual is a control signal, not a certificate.

6.2 Hypotheses this inventory generates

Each hypothesis names the ingredient it borrows, what would falsify it, and its relation to the program’s existing evidence. The order is by expected information per unit cost.

  1. Global constraint by solve, local prediction by network (borrowed from projection methods). Predict the velocity or a surface quantity pointwise, then obtain pressure by solving the constraint it must satisfy (a Poisson solve for divergence-free velocity in the interior; for a surface-only task, a global consistency solve is not available, which is itself informative about why surface pressure is the hard target). Falsifier: on the DrivAerML volume task, pressure from a Poisson solve of the predicted velocity is no more accurate than the directly predicted pressure. Evidence bearing on it: ISLA’s interior velocity is the field it predicts best (0.64x GeoTransolver-volume); if the constraint solve turns that into a pressure advantage, the pointwise-pressure gap closes for a reason the mechanism names. Cost: one sparse solve per case at inference, no training change; can be run as an eval-only study on saved volume predictions if the sampled interior points support a discretization, otherwise on a coarse background grid with stated interpolation error.

  2. Wall law as an architectural prior (borrowed from wall models). The program’s eddy-viscosity gap and the audit’s wall-flux spectrum argument both say the near-wall region needs resolution the interior does not. A surrogate that predicts wall shear through a learned local law of wall distance, local velocity and local pressure gradient, with the same law on every wall, would transfer that law across families. Falsifier: a local law fitted on DrivAerML does not reduce wall-shear error on the SHIFT-SUV fastback relative to a direct prediction. This is a component evaluation with target labels used only for scoring.

  3. Near-field / far-field split with analytic translation (borrowed from fast multipole). The composition study (Chapter 4) tests whether local responses compose; the exterior variant asks whether far-field interactions between separated bodies can be analytic while only near-field maps are learned. Falsifier: on the five-sphere family, a truncated multipole coupling with exact local responses fails to reproduce the reference pressure difference the audit resolved (0.005 in pressure coefficient) at an order that stays fixed as bodies move apart.

  4. Residual as a control signal (borrowed from iterative solvers). Even without a certificate, a residual of an available approximate operator (potential flow with a wake model, or a coarse RANS operator) evaluated on the surrogate’s prediction may rank cases by difficulty and flag failures. Falsifier: the residual’s correlation with the true per-case error is below 0.5 on held-out families, or no better than the model’s own seed disagreement (which the program measured at +0.67 in-family at 35 cases and near zero at 1,260). The value is triage, not accuracy.

  5. Learned smoother or prolongation on a classical hierarchy (borrowed from multigrid, the surviving form of the audit’s fourth direction after Chapter 5). The coarse space stays geometric; the network learns the part classical methods choose heuristically (smoother weights, prolongation stencils) conditioned on local operator entries. Prerequisite gate: exhibit an operator class where the classical hierarchy degrades (anisotropy, high Péclet, a state-dependent Jacobian); without such a failure there is nothing to learn.

  6. Continuation instead of extrapolation (borrowed from Newton and continuation methods). Regime extrapolation fails for every architecture tested; a solver would not extrapolate across stall but continue from the nearest solved angle. A surrogate conditioned on a nearby solution (the audit’s state-conditioned extension) rather than on the condition alone is the learned analogue. Falsifier: conditioning on the field at the nearest in-distribution angle does not reduce the post-stall error below the unconditioned surrogate’s. This needs volume or surface fields at the neighbouring condition at inference, which changes the product.

  7. Conservation by construction. A surrogate whose surface pressure and shear integrate to forces that satisfy a momentum balance with a predicted wake deficit would carry a physical constraint no current model enforces. Falsifier: enforcing the balance as a hard or soft constraint does not reduce force error on held-out families. This is the weakest of the seven as a transfer mechanism, because the constraint is a single scalar relation per case.

6.3 Priority and what runs first

Hypotheses 1 and 4 are eval-only on artifacts that exist and are queued behind the controls. Hypothesis 2 is a two-lane component evaluation and a natural companion to the program’s eddy-viscosity work. Hypothesis 3 rides on the composition study’s exterior variant. Hypotheses 5 through 7 are gated on prerequisites (a failing classical hierarchy; neighbouring-condition fields at inference; a conservation relation with predictive content) and are recorded here so that they are not rediscovered later without their falsifiers.
