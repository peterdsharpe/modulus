1  Controls before experiments

The DrivAerML → SHIFT-SUV zero-shot failure is a spatial field error, not a pressure-reference offset; the transfer configurations’ normalizations are source-only in effect for the reported metrics; and on an exactly specified paired-condition problem an omitted physical condition costs an architecture-independent floor that a per-point condition input repairs. These three controls were the audit’s prerequisites for any architectural experiment on transfer, because each names a way a transfer comparison can be wrong before it says anything about a model.

1.1 C1: offset or spatial error

A model that predicts a sensible field about the wrong constant has a relative L2 error near 1.0, which is where ISLA’s source-only fastback error sat (0.99 against GeoTransolver’s 0.59, three seeds each). DrivAerML and SHIFT-SUV are simulated with different ground and wheel conditions, freestream speeds and solvers, and pressure is nondimensionalized by each case’s dynamic pressure with no shift, so a reference difference between the datasets would appear as a per-case constant. The diagnostic decomposes each arm’s error, from the saved zero-shot predictions, into the area-weighted mean (the offset) and the centered remainder, and checks whether the offset cancels from the integrated pressure force, as it must on a closed surface with consistent normals (Section 2 for the preregistered bars).

The constant carries 1.5% (ISLA) and 3.0% (GeoTransolver) of the source-only squared error by the median over cases, against a 20% floor for the “spatial” band; removing it changes the relative error by under 1%, and the in-family arms pass the instrument check with offset shares under 1%. The offsets that do exist are small and of the same sign for both models (+0.14 of the true field’s RMS), consistent with a modest reference difference that explains none of the gap. The failure is a field error, and it is a force error: 1.6x (ISLA) and 1.4x (GeoTransolver) of the true force magnitude against 0.04 in-family, falling to 0.28 and 0.20 when the estate family joins training. Two consequences: the pressure-reference hypothesis is closed, and every transfer claim in this program reports the force error beside the field error, since a field error of 0.9 is a force error above 100%. What remains as the leading non-architectural explanation is the compound physical-condition mismatch between the datasets, which C3 tests in its exact form.

1.2 C2: source-only normalization

Both transfer configurations nondimensionalize pressure and wall shear by each case’s own dynamic pressure with no shift, and normalize wall shear by a declared per-dataset constant (standard deviation 0.00313 for DrivAerML, 0.00183 for SHIFT-SUV, zero mean). Inference denormalizes predictions with the statistics saved at training time, which are the source’s, and the relative L2 metric is invariant to a common scale, so the differing wall-shear constants do not bias the reported relative errors; they would bias an absolute or mixed-field loss, and a zero-shot protocol should carry the source’s constant into the target configuration. Pressure carries no constant beyond the physical nondimensionalization, so C1 is not a normalization artifact. Verdict: source-only in effect for the reported metrics, with one declared-constant hygiene rule (Section 5).

1.3 C3: a paired physical condition on an exact problem

The audit’s identifiability argument says that a model which does not see a physical condition faces an irreducible floor when that condition varies across cases of identical geometry, and that the floor is a property of the information, not the architecture. C3 measured it on exterior potential flow: 41 bodies (40 superellipsoids and the unit sphere, about 2,080 surface cells each) in free air and at 0.6 body half-heights above a ground plane, labels from a method-of-fundamental-solutions reference qualified against the sphere’s analytic pressure (error 4 × 10⁻⁵), with the ground effect changing the pressure field by 15% RMS (8–28% across bodies); 38 bodies pass the per-case residual and refinement gates. A small ISLA (hidden 64, four layers, 32 slices) trained three seeds on 28 shapes and was scored on nine held-out shapes and the sphere.

The repair is confirmed as preregistered: with the condition as a per-point input, the error on held-out shapes is 1.14x (free air) and 1.46x (ground) the in-distribution reference, inside the 1.5x bar. The floor is real and architecture-independent: the condition-blind ISLA sits at 1.31x the analytic floor and the multilayer perceptron at 3.0x, and 78% of the blind model’s squared error on ground cases falls in the lowest height quarter (57–88% across shapes; 25% would be uniform), so the model predicts a free-air-like field and pays where the physics differs rather than averaging the two conditions. One preregistration miscalibration is recorded rather than rewritten: the composite “floor confirmed” wording also demanded ≥ 3x the in-distribution error, but with the omitted condition adding the floor in quadrature the expected ratio at the measured reference is 2.15x, so that clause could not have been met; the measured 2.49x is the informative number (Section 12).

What this licenses about the DrivAerML → SHIFT-SUV shift: an omitted physical condition on identical geometry produces an error of the paired-label size that training on both conditions does not remove and a per-point condition input does, with a spatial signature at the location of the changed physics (near the ground and wheels, on real cars). It does not say how much of the observed 0.99 and 0.59 zero-shot errors the ground and wheel motion, speed and solver differences cause; that needs paired simulations on identical bodies, which do not exist, and the 15% field change here is small next to a multi-factor shift with separation.
