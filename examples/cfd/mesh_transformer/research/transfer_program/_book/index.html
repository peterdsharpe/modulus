Transfer program: from the ISLA audit to testable mechanisms

Author

Peter Sharpe

What this program tests

Thesis. The audit of the ISLA (Invariant Slice Attention) research record (the companion review in research/cross_dataset_generalization/, an untracked directory whose rendered report is _book/index.html there) concluded that the record does not establish a cross-dataset accuracy advantage, and that the promising targets are a complete physical input, a stable computational representation, and reusable components of the physical response. This program turns that agenda into experiments with preregistered bars, in the order the audit recommended: first the controls that make any later result interpretable (paired physical conditions, source-only normalization, and a pressure-offset versus spatial-error decomposition), then, in parallel, the computational-support study on the existing DrivAerML volume task and the nonlearned gates for the three response-based directions (boundary-response rank and basis transfer, local-response composition, solver correction spaces). A separate chapter asks what classical solvers do that learned surrogates do not, as a source of further hypotheses.

Utility. The quantity that matters is field and force error on geometries and dataset families outside the training distribution, with the PDE, boundary conditions, dimensionless parameters and label convention stated; with query and mesh consistency; and with the total cost counted (preprocessing, target-operator access, iterations, adaptation labels). A result that improves a fixed-mesh in-family score without moving that quantity is not progress here.

How to read this. Every chapter states current knowledge and the preregistered bar that would change it; the dated record of what was tried, in what order, is the notebook. Numbers in chapters are computed from checked-in artifacts under studies/. Verdicts follow bars written before the readout; amendments to an instrument are recorded before its numbers are read. Architectures are named (ISLA, GeoTransolver, Transolver); ratios are baseline error divided by ISLA error unless a chapter says otherwise.
